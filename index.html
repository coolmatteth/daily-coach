The two questions it answers

Am I eating enough to keep my muscle?

On a GLP-1 the hard part is not discipline, it is appetite. You get three protein rungs and a calorie band computed from your answers, with the formula named. Smaller sittings, more of them. Eat when hungry. The weekly average is what counts, not any one day.

Am I keeping my strength?

Lifting two to four days a week and keeping protein up are the two things you control, and in the trials they are what moved the share of weight lost as fat. The first plan is two days of full body on machines and dumbbells. A weight steps up only after you have owned it twice.

Doesn't Noom do this for free?

Some of it, yes, and it is good. Three things are different here.

  • No app to open. The plan arrives. You answer with numbers.
  • Real loads, earned. Not a video library. A weight goes up after you have owned it twice, and never before.
  • A verdict, not a dashboard. One Sunday message says how the week went and what to bring to your prescriber.

What it never does

  • Tell you what to do with your dose. Your prescriber decides that. The coach hands you your own numbers in their vocabulary.
  • Make a medical claim. It helps you keep protein up, train consistently and see your trends.
  • Show ads or sell your data. No trackers, no pixels.
  • Keep anything you do not want kept. Send "delete me" and it is gone.

How it works

  1. Three questions, then one a day. How many days you can lift, your weight, your medication. Your numbers come back that minute. After that, at most one question a day, each with the reason it buys you.
  2. One message every evening. Tomorrow's session with loads, or a rest day with a one-line check-in: stomach and energy, 1 to 5.
  3. Reply with what happened. Sets and weights after the gym. Meals by text or photo as you eat them. Fix anything in plain words.
  4. Sunday, the verdict. Protein, calories, trend, lifts, side-effect days, and a short summary you can read to your prescriber.
